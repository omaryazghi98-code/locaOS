# locaOS — Fieldwork, Validation & Funding Plan

**Purpose:** Persistent work queue for the non-coding work required to determine whether locaOS is commercially viable, fundable, adoptable, and worth taking to production.

> This document exists because product discovery cannot live only in chat. Every AI or human working on locaOS should check it before assuming that more coding is the next step.

## 1. Current strategic reality

locaOS is not only an engineering project. We still need to validate:

- the business opportunity
- the target customer profile
- willingness to change existing workflows
- willingness to pay
- actual operational pain
- the best initial market segment
- the appropriate go-to-market motion
- legal/regulatory constraints
- funding eligibility in Morocco
- realistic development/operating costs
- which expenses should be grant/subsidy funded versus investor funded versus bootstrapped/revenue funded

**Current available cash for the project: effectively $0.** Funding availability is therefore a first-class constraint and research topic.

Do not assume that because a program exists, locaOS qualifies. Eligibility must be verified against the current official program rules, applicant status, company stage, geography, sector, expenses, co-financing requirements, deadlines, and documentation requirements.

## 2. Workstream A — Customer discovery / fieldwork

### Objective

Observe how rental agencies actually operate before locking the product around assumptions.

The preferred method is **shadowing and direct observation**, supplemented by structured interviews.

### Who to study

Prioritize a representative range:

1. Medium rental agencies.
2. Higher-end / professionally managed agencies.
3. Agencies with multiple branches or meaningful fleet size.
4. Agencies with formal reservation/contract/finance processes.
5. Smaller local agencies as a contrast group.
6. Owners.
7. General managers / operations managers.
8. Rental agents/front-desk staff.
9. Fleet/field staff.
10. Accounting/finance staff where separate.

### Important current hypothesis

An expert advised that many very small local agencies, particularly around Tetouan, may be highly phone/WhatsApp-driven and intentionally operate with minimal process. They may resist changing methodology and therefore may be poor initial ICP targets.

**This is a hypothesis, not a proven fact.** Fieldwork must test it.

Current strategic hypothesis:

> **Initial ICP may be medium-to-high maturity rental agencies that already feel operational pain and have enough process complexity to value a system.**

Do not turn this into a permanent segmentation decision until validated through interviews and observed behavior.

## 3. Shadowing protocol

For each agency, attempt to observe a real rental lifecycle:

`Inquiry → availability check → quote → customer qualification → reservation → vehicle assignment → contract → deposit → departure inspection → handover → active rental → extension/problem → return → inspection → settlement → payment/deposit release → vehicle preparation → next availability`

Record what actually happens, not what the person says should happen.

Observe:

- phone calls
- WhatsApp usage
- spreadsheets/notebooks
- calendars
- paper contracts
- payment/deposit handling
- cash handling
- bank transfers
- card payments
- vehicle keys
- vehicle status tracking
- mileage/fuel capture
- inspection photos
- damage handling
- customer identity documents
- additional drivers
- extras/add-ons
- late returns
- extensions
- cancellations/no-shows
- vehicle swaps
- one-way rentals
- delivery/pickup
- airport/hotel operations
- maintenance coordination
- washing/preparation
- fleet relocation
- accounting/invoicing
- reporting to owner/manager
- customer complaints
- staff handoffs
- exceptions and emergencies

## 4. Questions to answer through fieldwork

### Pain

- What wastes the most staff time?
- What causes lost revenue?
- What causes disputes with customers?
- Where do mistakes happen?
- What information gets lost between phone/WhatsApp/paper/spreadsheets?
- What happens when the owner/manager is absent?
- What is hardest to know in real time?

### Money

- How are rates calculated?
- How are duration discounts handled?
- How is mileage handled?
- How are deposits secured/released?
- How are extras charged?
- How are damage/fuel/late charges calculated?
- How are refunds handled?
- How are cash/bank/card payments reconciled?
- What financial reports does the owner actually use?

### Operations

- How do they know which vehicle is available?
- How do they know which vehicles need washing/prep?
- How do they track maintenance downtime?
- How are inspections performed?
- How are damages evidenced?
- How are extensions approved?
- How are overdue vehicles followed up?

### Adoption

- What software do they already use?
- What do they hate about it?
- Why would they refuse a new system?
- What would make switching worth the effort?
- Would they tolerate data entry?
- Who decides to buy?
- Who actually uses the system daily?
- What onboarding effort is acceptable?
- Would they prefer phone/WhatsApp-first workflows even with a proper system underneath?

### Willingness to pay

Do not ask only “Would you pay for this?”

Investigate:

- current software spend
- staff time spent on manual processes
- cost of operational mistakes
- lost bookings
- uncollected charges
- vehicle downtime
- accounting effort
- acceptable monthly price
- acceptable per-vehicle price
- acceptable transaction fee
- willingness to pay for customer website/booking
- willingness to pay for automation/integrations
- willingness to pay for premium support

## 9. What should government funding pay for?

This must be determined program by program, but the strategic preference is to use non-dilutive/public support where it legitimately funds:

- product R&D
- software development
- innovation/technology work
- prototyping
- research/validation
- eligible equipment
- eligible hiring/training
- digital transformation activities
- cybersecurity/compliance work
- eligible experimentation
- market validation where permitted
- eligible internationalization/market access

Do **not** artificially classify ordinary operating expenses as innovation merely to obtain funding.

## 10. What should investors fund?

Investor capital is better suited to activities where speed, growth and market expansion matter, for example:

- hiring senior engineering/product talent
- product commercialization
- sales and business development
- customer acquisition
- onboarding/support
- expansion beyond the initial region
- infrastructure scaling
- working capital where appropriate
- strategic partnerships
- expansion of the customer-facing ecosystem
- building a defensible technology/data/operations platform

Investment should not be raised merely because we have not finished validating whether agencies want the product.

## 11. Bootstrap/revenue-funded expenses

Where possible, reserve customer revenue for:

- hosting
- domain/email
- routine SaaS tools
- support
- small operational expenses
- travel/fieldwork when feasible
- incremental infrastructure
- maintenance

At the current $0 starting point, minimize recurring costs until funding or revenue exists.

## 12. Funding strategy principle

Do not ask “How do we get money for locaOS?”

Ask:

> **Which source of capital is structurally appropriate for each work package?**

For every major expense, classify:

`Grant/subsidy | Guarantee/loan | Investor | Founder/bootstrapped | Customer revenue | Partner-funded | Not yet justified`

Then record why.

## 15. Adoption strategy hypothesis

Potential positioning should not be:

> “Replace everything you currently do.”

For resistant agencies, investigate lower-friction entry points such as:

- reservations + availability
- contracts/documents
- fleet visibility
- payments/deposits
- customer website/booking
- inspections
- reporting

The product may need to coexist with WhatsApp and phone operations rather than demanding an immediate behavior change.

This is a hypothesis to validate, not a final GTM decision.

## 16. Research ethics / privacy

Fieldwork should:

- obtain permission before recording
- avoid collecting unnecessary personal data
- anonymize agency/customer identities in research notes where possible
- never copy customer identity documents into research files unless there is an explicit lawful/necessary process
- distinguish observation from interpretation
- distinguish participant claims from verified facts
- store consent/permission notes where required

## 18. Hiring decision gate

A real senior developer should be brought in when one of these is true:

- a funded development package is approved/likely enough to justify hiring;
- customer validation has established a sufficiently stable MVP scope;
- the founder has enough runway to manage development properly;
- a technical audit identifies work that requires production-grade human engineering.

Before hiring, give the developer the repository memory documents and require a technical audit rather than immediately asking them to rewrite the project.

## 19. Evidence standard

Every major strategic decision should eventually have one of:

- direct field observation
- customer interview evidence
- signed/credible partner signal
- official government/program documentation
- competitor evidence
- financial/accounting evidence
- technical benchmark/test
- clearly labeled hypothesis

Do not turn repeated AI speculation into “market validation.”

## 20. Permanent reminder

There are two parallel products being built:

**The software** — rental operations, money, fleet, documents, customer experience, integrations and future ecosystem.

**The company** — validated market, ICP, business model, funding strategy, legal/compliance position, go-to-market, hiring plan and economics.

Both must progress.

> **Do not spend money building an unvalidated assumption. Do not delay validation because coding feels easier. Do not forget an idea simply because it is not the current sprint.**
